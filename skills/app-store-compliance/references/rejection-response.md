# Rejection Response Structure

## Rejection Analysis

When the user provided a rejection reason in Phase 2, append the following section to the compliance report:

---

### Rejection Response

**Rejection Analysis**
1. **Exact rejection reason:** [quote verbatim from Apple/Google]
2. **Guideline violated:** [section number and title — e.g., "Guideline 3.1.1 – Business – Payments – In-App Purchase"]
3. **What the reviewer likely saw:** [explain what triggered the rejection based on app details from Phase 2]
4. **Steps to resolve:**
   - [Step 1]
   - [Step 2]
   - [Step 3]
5. **Appeal recommended?** [Yes if rejection appears overly broad or incorrect — include appeal message below. No if violation is clear.]

---

## Appeal Message Template

Use only if the rejection appears incorrect or the policy language is ambiguous.

```
Subject: Appeal: [App Name] — [Rejection Reason Code, e.g., Guideline 3.1.1]

Dear App Review Team,

Thank you for reviewing [App Name]. We would like to respectfully appeal the rejection under guideline [X.X].

[Explain what the app does and why it does not violate the cited guideline — cite specific features and behaviors. OR: explain exactly what change was made to resolve the issue.]

[If changes were made:]
We have made the following changes in version [X.X.X]:
- [Change 1]
- [Change 2]

[If providing additional context:]
We would be happy to provide a screen recording or additional documentation demonstrating [specific feature or workflow].

Thank you for your time and consideration.

[Name]
[Developer/Company Name]
[Contact email]
```

## Notes on Appeals

- Apple appeals: submit via App Store Connect → App → Resolution Center. Escalate unresolved appeals to the App Review Board.
- Google Play appeals: submit via the Play Console notification or the policy appeals form. Provide the exact policy cited and your counter-argument.
- Lead with facts, not frustration. Cite specific guideline language and explain why your app's behavior does not fall within its scope.
- If changes were made: list them explicitly — reviewers are looking for evidence of compliance, not assurances.
- Include a test account if the rejection involved content the reviewer couldn't access.
